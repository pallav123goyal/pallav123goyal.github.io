Note: An isometry is a transformation that preserves distances between points. These problems explore how isometries are determined by their action on specific points and how to classify them.

Problem 1 (3 + 3 + 2 + 4 + 2 points)

Suppose \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\) are points in the plane which do not lie in a line. Let \(f\) be an isometry.

(a) Explain why \(f(A)\), \(f(B)\), and \(f(C)\) cannot lie in a line.

(b) What is the relationship between \(\Delta f(A)f(B)f(C)\) and \(\Delta ABC\)?

(c) Suppose two points \(P\) and \(Q\) are equidistant from \(A\), so \(|AP| = |AQ|\). What does this mean about the point \(A\) in relationship to \(P\) and \(Q\)?

(d) Suppose \(P\) and \(Q\) are also equidistant from \(B\) and from \(C\). What does this say about \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\)? (Hint: Because of our starting assumption, this means that \(P = Q\), so a point \(P\) is uniquely determined by its distances from three points which don't lie in a line.)

(e) Let \(P\) be a point for which you know the distances to \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\). What is the relationship between \(f(P)\) and \(f(A)\), \(f(B)\), and \(f(C)\)?

Conclusion: You have now shown that an isometry \(f\) is completely determined by where it sends three points \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\).

Problem 2 (3 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 4 points)

Consider the isometry defined by the formula:

\[f(x,y) = \left( \frac{-3}{5}x + \frac{4}{5}y, \frac{4}{5}x + \frac{3}{5}y \right)\]

(a) Compute \(f(1,0)\), \(f(0,0)\), and \(f(0,1)\).

(b) Compute the fixed points of \(f\).

(c) Using part (b), describe what the transformation \(f\) is (e.g., translation, rotation, reflection).

(d) What is the relationship between the line segment \((1,0)f(1,0)\) and the line \(y=2x\)?

(e) Can you say anything in general about the relationship between the line segment \(Pf(P)\) and the line \(y=2x\)?

Problem 3 (3 + 2 + 2 points)

Consider the transformation \(f(x,y) = (3x+y, -y+2)\).

(a) Compute the fixed points of \(f\).

(b) Is \(f\) an isometry?

(c) Is \(f\) a translation, rotation, reflection, glide reflection, or none of these? Justify your answer.

Problem 4 (4 + 4 points)

Let \(A=(0,1)\), \(B=(0,0)\), and \(C=(1,0)\). Suppose \(f\) is an isometry.

(a) Suppose that \(f(A)=(1.8,3)\), \(f(B)=(1.8,2)\) and \(f(C)=(2.8,2)\). Is \(f\) a translation, a reflection, or a rotation? Give the formula for \(f\).

(b) Suppose \(f(A)=(0.4,1.8)\), \(f(B)=(1,1)\) and \(f(C)=(1.8,1.6)\). Explain why the isometry \(f\) is not a translation or a reflection. (Hint: Problem 2(e) may help.)

Problem 5 (6 points)

Write down 3 facts that you know from linear algebra and explain them in a couple of sentences each. These could be from Math 31, Math 131, or elsewhere. If any of the facts have a geometrical significance, feel free to describe a picture to explain.
