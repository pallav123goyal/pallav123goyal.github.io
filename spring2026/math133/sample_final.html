2. True or False Questions (8 points each)

For each of the following statements, determine if it is true or false, and provide a brief proof or counterexample for your claim. Credit on these questions is based on your justification.

(a) Segment Constructibility

Given a line segment of length \(1\), a line segment of length \(\sqrt{\pi}+\frac{3}{2}\) is constructible using compass and straightedge.

(b) Linear Composition

The composition of any two linear transformations of \(\mathbb{R}^{2}\) is an affine transformation of \(\mathbb{R}^{2}.\)

(c) Isometries

Dilation is an isometry in Hyperbolic geometry.

(d) Set Mapping

The transformation \(f(x,y)=\left(\cos\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right)x-\sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right)y, \; \sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right)x+\cos\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right)y\right)\) sends the set of points \((x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\) satisfying the equation \((x-3)^{2}+(y+1)^{2}=49\) to a line in \(\mathbb{R}^2\).

3. Short Answer / Constructions

(a) Geometric Segment Construction (14 points)

Given line segments \(AB\) and \(CD\) that are not parallel or perpendicular to each other and do not intersect, construct a line segment of length \(|CD| - |AB|\). List all your steps.

You may use the following tools:

  • Can draw a circle of radius \(r\) with center \(Z\) (Compass Axiom 3)
  • Can draw a straight line through any two points and can extend any line segment (Straightedge Axioms 1–2)
  • Can draw a parallel line to a line through a point \(P\) (Axiom 5)
  • Can draw a perpendicular line to a line \(L\) through a point \(P\) (the point \(P\) can be on the line or not)
  • Can bisect an angle

Justify why your construction works correctly.

(b) Transformation Analysis 1 (12 points)

Let \(f\left(\begin{bmatrix}x\\ y\end{bmatrix}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}-1&1\\ 0&-1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\ y\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}2\\ 0\end{bmatrix}\) be a transformation of \(\mathbb{R}^{2}.\)

  1. Compute \(f(1,2)\) and \(f(-3,4)\).
  2. Determine whether \(f\) is an isometry.
  3. Find all fixed points of \(f\).
  4. Is \(f\) a translation, reflection, rotation, glide reflection, or none of the above?
(c) Transformation Analysis 2 (12 points)

Let \(f(x,y)=(-y+2,-x-2)\) be a transformation of \(\mathbb{R}^{2}.\)

  1. Compute \(f(1,2)\) and \(f(-3,4)\).
  2. Determine whether \(f\) is an isometry.
  3. Find all fixed points of \(f\).
  4. Is \(f\) a translation, reflection, rotation, glide reflection, or none of the above?

4. Short Proofs

(a) Quadrilateral Properties (14 points)

In this problem, you will show that if opposite sides of a quadrilateral \(ABCD\) have the same length, then the quadrilateral is a parallelogram. You can use any results from class, worksheets, or the midterm.

  1. Explain why \(\Delta ABC \cong \Delta CDA\).
  2. Explain why \(\angle CBA + \angle BAD = \pi\).
  3. Explain why you can conclude that \(BC \parallel AD\).
  4. Determine what properties complete the proof of this statement.
(b) Midpoint Coordinates (6 points)

Consider two points \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\) in the Cartesian plane. Show that the midpoint of these points has coordinates \(\left(\frac{a+c}{2}, \frac{b+d}{2}\right)\).

(c) Circle Tangency (6 points)

Show that the line \(3x+4y-5=0\) is tangent to the circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}=1\).

(d) Hyperbolic distance (6 points)

Find the distance between the points \((2,3)\) and \((2,5)\) in the hyperbolic plane.
