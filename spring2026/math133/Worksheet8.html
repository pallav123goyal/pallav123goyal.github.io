Problem 6

(2 + 2 + 2 + 2 points) The Euclidean length \(l_{\mathbb{R}}(\gamma)\) of a curve \(\gamma\) in \(\mathbb{R}^{2}\), parametrized by the differentiable vector-valued function \(\gamma(t) = (x(t), y(t))\) for \(a \le t \le b\) is the arc length, given by the formula:

\[l_{\mathbb{R}}(\gamma) = \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{[x'(t)]^{2} + [y'(t)]^{2}} \, dt\]

FACT: This length is independent of the parametrization.

  1. Consider the curve \(\gamma(t) = (\cos(t), \sin(t))\) for \0 \le t \le 2\pi\). Describe the curve and compute its Euclidean length.
  2. Consider the curve \(\gamma(t) = (2t+1, 3t-2)\) for \(-1 \le t \le 2\). Describe the curve and compute its Euclidean length.
  3. What sort of curve is \(\gamma(t) = (ct, dt)\), where \(c\) and \(d\) are real numbers? Compute its Euclidean length for \(a \le t \le b\).
  4. Compute the distance from \(\gamma(a)\) to \(\gamma(b)\) in part (c) using the definition of distance between two points given in class (the distance formula). How does this compare to your answer in (c)?

FACT: The line segment \(PQ\) is the shortest curve going from \(P\) to \(Q\) in the Cartesian plane! See the optional exercise at the end for a proof.

Problem 7

(3 + 3 + 3 points) Let \(\mathbb{H}^{2}\) denote the Cartesian plane \(\mathbb{R}^{2}\) with \(y > 0\). The hyperbolic length \(l_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma)\) of a curve \(\gamma\) in \(\mathbb{H}^{2}\) parametrized by the differentiable vector-valued function \(\gamma(t) = (x(t), y(t))\) for \(a \le t \le b\) is defined by the formula:

\[l_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma) = \int_{a}^{b} \frac{\sqrt{[x'(t)]^{2} + [y'(t)]^{2}}}{y(t)} \, dt\]
  1. Consider the curve \(\gamma(t) = (\cos(t), \sin(t))\) for \(\pi/4 \le t \le 3\pi/4\). Describe the curve and compute its hyperbolic length. (You can look up the integral.)
  2. Consider the curve \(\gamma(t) = \left(t, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\) for \(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \le t \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\). Describe the curve and compute its hyperbolic length.
  3. Consider the curve \(\gamma(t) = (2,t)\) for \(1 \le t \le 2\). Describe the curve and compute its hyperbolic length.

FACT: The line segment \(PQ\) is not always the shortest curve between the points \(P\) and \(Q\) using hyperbolic length!

Optional Exercise

The goal of this exercise is to rigorously prove that the line segment \(PQ\) is the shortest curve from point \(P\) to point \(Q\) in Euclidean geometry. Consider a differentiable curve \(\gamma\) from \(P\) to \(Q\). We want to show that the Euclidean length \(l_{\mathbb{R}}(\gamma)\) defined in problem 6 is greater than or equal to the Euclidean length of the line segment \(PQ\).

  1. Let \(P = (x_{1}, y_{1})\) and let \(Q = (x_{1}, y_{2})\), so \(PQ\) lies on the vertical line of equation \(x = x_{1}\). Show that the Euclidean length \(l_{\mathbb{R}}(\gamma)\) is greater than or equal to \(|y_{2} - y_{1}|\), the Euclidean length of the line segment \(PQ\).
  2. In the general case, let \(f(x,y) = (\cos(\theta)x - \sin(\theta)y, \, \sin(\theta)x + \cos(\theta)y)\) be a rotation such that \(f(P)\) and \(f(Q)\) lie on the same vertical line. Show that the curve \(f(\gamma)\) from \(f(P)\) to \(f(Q)\) has the same Euclidean length as \(\gamma\).
  3. Combine parts (a) and (b) to conclude that \(l_{\mathbb{R}}(\gamma) \ge l_{\mathbb{R}}(PQ)\).
