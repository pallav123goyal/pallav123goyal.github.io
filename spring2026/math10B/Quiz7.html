Problem 1 (5 points)

Set up the surface integral for the function \(f(x,y,z) = e^x + y^2 z\) on the surface of the graph of the function \(g(x,y) = x^3 + e^y\) over the rectangle \((x,y) \in [0,2] \times [0,3]\). Find the surface differential element by parameterizing in terms of \(x\) and \(y\).

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Parameterize the surface

We choose \(x\) and \(y\) as our parameters to describe the surface \(z = x^3 + e^y\). The vector parameterization equation \(\mathbf{r}(x,y)\) representing any point on this surface is:

\[\mathbf{r}(x,y) = x\mathbf{\hat{i}} + y\mathbf{\hat{j}} + (x^3 + e^y)\mathbf{\hat{k}}\]

The limits for our parameters are defined directly by the rectangular region: \(x \in [0,2]\) and \(y \in [0,3]\).

Step 2: Compute the tangent vectors

To construct the normal vector, we find the partial derivatives of our parameterization vector with respect to each independent variable:

\[\mathbf{r}_x = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial x} = 1\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 0\mathbf{\hat{j}} + 3x^2\mathbf{\hat{k}}\] \[\mathbf{r}_y = \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial y} = 0\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 1\mathbf{\hat{j}} + e^y\mathbf{\hat{k}}\]
Step 3: Calculate the normal vector via the cross product

The standard cross product of the tangent vectors yields a vector perpendicular to the surface:

\[\mathbf{r}_x \times \mathbf{r}_y = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{\hat{i}} & \mathbf{\hat{j}} & \mathbf{\hat{k}} \\ 1 & 0 & 3x^2 \\ 0 & 1 & e^y \end{vmatrix}\] \[= \mathbf{\hat{i}}(0 - 3x^2) - \mathbf{\hat{j}}(e^y - 0) + \mathbf{\hat{k}}(1 - 0)\] \[= -3x^2\mathbf{\hat{i}} - e^y\mathbf{\hat{j}} + 1\mathbf{\hat{k}}\]
Step 4: Construct the magnitude of the normal vector for the scalar surface element

Since we are evaluating a scalar surface integral, we require the magnitude of the cross product vector to find our differential area component \(dS = \|\mathbf{r}_x \times \mathbf{r}_y\| \, dy \, dx\):

\[\|\mathbf{r}_x \times \mathbf{r}_y\| = \sqrt{(-3x^2)^2 + (-e^y)^2 + (1)^2} = \sqrt{9x^4 + e^{2y} + 1}\] \[dS = \sqrt{1 + 9x^4 + e^{2y}} \, dy \, dx\]
Step 5: Substitute the parameterized variables into the integrand and set up the limits

We transform the target function \(f(x,y,z) = e^x + y^2 z\) into the parameter space by replacing \(z\) with its definition along the surface, \(z = x^3 + e^y\):

\[f(\mathbf{r}(x,y)) = e^x + y^2(x^3 + e^y)\]

Combining the converted integrand, the parameterized magnitude factor, and the rectangular coordinate boundaries, the full surface integral becomes:

\[\int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{3} \left(e^x + y^2(x^3 + e^y)\right) \sqrt{1 + 9x^4 + e^{2y}} \, dy \, dx\]

Problem 2 (5 points)

Set up the surface integral for the function \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = x^2 y \mathbf{\hat{i}} + e^y \mathbf{\hat{j}} + \sin(xy) \mathbf{\hat{k}}\) on the cylinder \(x^2 + y^2 = 9\) between \(z = 2\) and \(z = 4\). Use the outward normal direction.

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Parameterize the cylindrical surface

The cylinder expression \(x^2 + y^2 = 9\) points to a fixed radius of \(r = 3\). We choose standard cylindrical coordinates \(\theta\) and \(z\) as our parameter space variables. The parameterization position vector \(\mathbf{r}(\theta, z)\) is:

\[\mathbf{r}(\theta, z) = 3\cos(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 3\sin(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{j}} + z\mathbf{\hat{k}}\]

The boundaries for these parameter constraints are defined by \(\theta \in [0, 2\pi]\) and \(z \in [2, 4]\).

Step 2: Find the normal tangent cross product vector

To compute the directed normal vector piece, we first calculate the respective partial derivative tangent vectors:

\[\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \theta} = -3\sin(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 3\cos(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{j}} + 0\mathbf{\hat{k}}\] \[\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial z} = 0\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 0\mathbf{\hat{j}} + 1\mathbf{\hat{k}}\]

Taking the cross product of these partials establishes our normal direction vector:

\[\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \theta} \times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial z} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{\hat{i}} & \mathbf{\hat{j}} & \mathbf{\hat{k}} \\ -3\sin(\theta) & 3\cos(\theta) & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 3\cos(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 3\sin(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{j}} + 0\mathbf{\hat{k}}\]

Notice that this normal orientation vector points directly outward away from the \(z\)-axis center line (since components match the signs of \(x\) and \(y\)), successfully complying with the "outward normal" requirement. Thus, \(d\mathbf{\mathbf{S}} = (3\cos(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{i}} + 3\sin(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{j}}) \, dz \, d\theta\).

Step 3: Evaluate the vector field \(\mathbf{F}\) on the surface

Substituting \(x = 3\cos(\theta)\), \(y = 3\sin(\theta)\), and \(z = z\) into the vector equation \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)\):

\[\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(\theta,z)) = (3\cos(\theta))^2(3\sin(\theta))\mathbf{\hat{i}} + e^{3\sin(\theta)}\mathbf{\hat{j}} + \sin(9\cos(\theta)\sin(\theta))\mathbf{\hat{k}}\] \[\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(\theta,z)) = 27\cos^2(\theta)\sin(\theta)\mathbf{\hat{i}} + e^{3\sin(\theta)}\mathbf{\hat{j}} + \sin(9\cos(\theta)\sin(\theta))\mathbf{\hat{k}}\]
Step 4: Compute the dot product \(\mathbf{F} \cdot \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \theta} \times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial z}\right)\)

Performing the algebraic dot product leaves us with a scalar expression ready for direct single-plane evaluation:

\[\mathbf{F} \cdot \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \theta} \times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial z}\right) = \left(27\cos^2(\theta)\sin(\theta)\right)(3\cos(\theta)) + \left(e^{3\sin(\theta)}\right)(3\sin(\theta)) + \left(\sin(9\cos(\theta)\sin(\theta))\right)(0)\] \[= 81\cos^3(\theta)\sin(\theta) + 3\sin(\theta)e^{3\sin(\theta)}\]
Step 5: Final double integral integration setup

Assembling limits and our dot product scalar together, the comprehensive surface integral setup becomes:

\[\int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{2}^{4} \left(81\cos^3(\theta)\sin(\theta) + 3\sin(\theta)e^{3\sin(\theta)}\right) \, dz \, d\theta\]
