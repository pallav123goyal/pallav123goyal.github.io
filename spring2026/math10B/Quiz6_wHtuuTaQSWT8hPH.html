Problem 1 (3 points)

Find a parametrization for the surface \(x^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}=1\).

Hint: The above equation can be rewritten as \(x^{2}-(y^{2}+z^{2})=1\). The \(y^{2}+z^{2}\) term should motivate some nice substitution.

Solution:

Following the hint, we rewrite the equation as:

\[x^{2} = 1 + (y^{2} + z^{2})\]

We notice that \(y^2 + z^2\) can be modeled as the cross-sectional radius squared. If we choose a trigonometric substitution for standard parameterized circles, we can set:

\[y = r\cos(v)\] \[z = r\sin(v)\]

Substituting these definitions back into our equation yields:

\[x^{2} = 1 + r^2\] \[x^{2} -r^2 = 1\]

Using the trigonometric identity \(\sec^2(u) - \tan^{2}(u) = 1\), we obtain:

\[x =\sec(u), r=\tan(u).\]

Therefore, the complete trigonometric vector function parameterization representing both sheets of the hyperboloid is given by:

\[\vec{r}(u, v) = \left(\sec(u),\, \tan(u)\cos(v),\, \tan(u)\sin(v)\right)\]

with the parameter domain limits bounded by \(u \in [0, \frac{\pi}{2}) \cup (\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi]\) and \(v \in [0, 2\pi]\).

Problem 2

Consider the parametrized surface \(S\) defined via \(\vec{r}(u,v) = (u+v, e^{u}, e^{v})\) for \(u\in[0,1]\) and \(v\in[0,2]\).

(a) (2 points) Find expressions for \(T_{u}\) and \(T_{v}\).

Solution:

The standard partial derivatives with respect to each parameter coordinate yield the tangent vectors:

\[T_{u} = \frac{\partial \vec{r}}{\partial u} = (1,\, e^{u},\, 0)\] \[T_{v} = \frac{\partial \vec{r}}{\partial v} = (1,\, 0,\, e^{v})\]

(b) (3 points) Compute the equation of the tangent plane to \(S\) at the point (1, e, 1).

Solution:

Step 1: Determine the target parameter values \((u, v)\)

To match the given coordinate point \((x, y, z) = (1, e, 1)\) with our parametrization \((u+v, e^{u}, e^{v})\), we construct the component system:

  1. \(u + v = 1\)
  2. \(e^{u} = e \implies u = 1\)
  3. \(e^{v} = 1 \implies v = 0\)

Verifying with the first equation shows \(1 + 0 = 1\), confirming that the point matches parameters \((u, v) = (1, 0)\).

Step 2: Evaluate the tangent vectors at \((1, 0)\)
\[T_{u}(1,0) = (1,\, e^{1},\, 0) = (1,\, e,\, 0)\] \[T_{v}(1,0) = (1,\, 0,\, e^{0}) = (1,\, 0,\, 1)\]
Step 3: Calculate the normal vector \(\vec{n}\)

The normal vector orthogonal to the plane is found by taking the cross product of our evaluated tangent vectors:

\[\vec{n} = T_{u}(1,0) \times T_{v}(1,0) = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 1 & e & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix}\] \[\vec{n} = \vec{i}(e \cdot 1 - 0) - \vec{j}(1 \cdot 1 - 0) + \vec{k}(0 - e \cdot 1) = (e,\, -1,\, -e)\]
Step 4: Establish the equation of the tangent plane

Using the point-normal definition of a plane \(a(x - x_0) + b(y - y_0) + c(z - z_0) = 0\) at the point \((1, e, 1)\):

\[e(x - 1) - 1(y - e) - e(z - 1) = 0\]

Expanding the linear distribution yields:

\[ex - e - y + e - ez + e = 0\]

Simplifying the values results in the standard form plane equation:

\[ex - y - ez = -e\]

(c) (2 points) Set up an integral to compute the area of the surface \(S\). (You don't need to evaluate it.)

Solution:

The standard definition mapping formula for finding surface area is given by:

\[\text{Area}(S) = \iint_D \|T_{u} \times T_{v}\| \,dA\]
Step 1: Compute the general cross product vector function
\[T_{u} \times T_{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 1 & e^{u} & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & e^{v} \end{vmatrix} = (e^{u}e^{v},\, -e^{v},\, -e^{u}) = (e^{u+v},\, -e^{v},\, -e^{u})\]
Step 2: Obtain the magnitude expression
\[\|T_{u} \times T_{v}\| = \sqrt{(e^{u+v})^{2} + (-e^{v})^{2} + (-e^{u})^{2}} = \sqrt{e^{2u+2v} + e^{2v} + e^{2u}}\]
Step 3: Set up the bounded iterated integral

Using the parameter region bounds given by \(u \in [0, 1]\) and \(v \in [0, 2]\), the complete double integral expression is:

\[\text{Area}(S) = \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{e^{2u+2v} + e^{2v} + e^{2u}} \,du\,dv\]
